01 / Research

Do learned detectors survive real-world change?

Research & publications{% include icon.html name="arrow-right" %}

Undergraduate thesis · journal paper under review

Temporal Generalization and Explanation Stability of Control Flow Graph Neural Networks for Malware Detection

Md. Asif Sajeed, Md. Nazrul Islam Mondal, Md Ashraful Hossen Akash

Malware detectors are usually tested on a random split of samples from one period, which says little about how they behave on the next year's binaries. I lifted Windows PE files into control flow graphs with angr, trained twelve graph neural network variants and a flat, topology-free baseline on samples from 2024-2025, and scored them once on a disjoint 2026 corpus.

05 / Background

Where security meets machine learning

I graduated from Rajshahi University of Engineering & Technology (RUET) in August 2026, ranked 14th in my cohort with a CGPA of 3.79/4.00. My undergraduate thesis built graph-neural-network malware detectors from binary control flow graphs, then tested them on malware collected a year later. The model that looked best on the usual benchmark was among the first to break, and that finding is now a journal paper under review.

Before the thesis I led a four-person undergraduate research group whose work on satellite imagery, clinical text and medical imaging produced three IEEE conference papers. Outside research I analyse malware, reverse engineer binaries and play CTFs, mostly solo on Hack The Box, and I write up the challenges I learn the most from. At the moment I'm working through Practical Malware Analysis and Hands-On Machine Learning.

Research interests

  • Malware detection under distribution shift
  • Binary analysis & reverse engineering
  • Explainability of security models
  • DFIR & malware analysis

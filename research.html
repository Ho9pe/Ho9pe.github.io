Temporal Generalization and Explanation Stability of Control Flow Graph Neural Networks for Malware Detection

Md. Asif Sajeed, Md. Nazrul Islam Mondal, Md Ashraful Hossen Akash · under review at the Journal of Information Security and Applications

arXiv:2609.24280{% include icon.html name="external" px=13 %} Code · TG-CFG{% include icon.html name="external" px=13 %}

Malware detectors are usually tested on a random split of samples from one period, which says little about how they behave on the next year's binaries. I built a fully static pipeline that lifts Windows PE files into control flow graphs with angr and encodes every basic block. I then trained twelve graph neural network variants and a flat, topology-free baseline on samples from 2024-2025 and scored them once on a disjoint 2026 corpus.

Key findings

010.932 → 0.807

Rank reversal

The flat baseline was among the best in-distribution models (AUC 0.932) but among the worst after the time boundary (0.807), while PNA held 0.879.

02Friedman p = 0.0006

The message-passing operator matters

Operator choice significantly changed robustness. Recalibration did not fix the gap, and ensembling fragile operators did not replace choosing a robust one.

03GNNExplainer

Explanations are architecture-specific

GNNExplainer attributions did not drift over time, but two architectures agreed on important blocks only at chance level.

04CFGNet-v2

A design derived from the findings

CFGNet-v2 matched the best searched operator without any search.
